Coral regrows 4x faster with the sound of a healthy reef

A field team ran underwater speakers over twelve damaged reef plots for one season, playing night-time recordings of a healthy reef: snapping shrimp, grazing fish. Twelve matched plots stayed silent.

Young coral larvae, which find a home partly by sound, settled on the playback plots at four times the rate of the silent ones (mean 41 versus 10 settlers per tile). The effect faded within 20 metres of a speaker.

The team says playback only helps where there is clean water and a source of larvae nearby; it doesn't fix a reef by itself. Next: a two-year trial to see whether the extra settlers survive.
