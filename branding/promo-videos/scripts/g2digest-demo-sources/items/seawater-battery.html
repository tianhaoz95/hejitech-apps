Seawater battery keeps its charge 3x longer

A preprint describes a battery cell that uses sea salt as its electrolyte. Earlier designs lost charge while idle because salt fouled the electrodes. A thin new membrane keeps it off: the cell lost 6% of its charge over 30 days idle, against 18% for the previous design.

The cells are small lab prototypes (coin cells). The membrane adds about 4% to the material cost. The team wants to test pouch cells next year; the work hasn't been peer reviewed yet.
