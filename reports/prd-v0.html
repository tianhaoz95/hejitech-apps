    AI-Native STR Material Participation Tracker

    A hands-free, audit-defensible hour and evidence log for short-term rental owners pursuing material participation tax status — built agent-first, not form-first.

    Status: Draft Version: v0 Date: July 14, 2026 Owner: Jackson Zhou Scope: Strategy & requirements, pre-MVP

    1. Problem Statement

    Short-term rental owners can reclassify rental losses as non-passive income — unlocking deductions that offset W-2 or business income — by satisfying an IRS material participation test (commonly the 100-hour or 500-hour test for STRs, or the 750-hour test for Real Estate Professional Status). The tax savings from qualifying are often five to six figures in year one.

    The strategy is well understood; the failure mode is not the tax concept, it's the paperwork. The IRS requires contemporaneous records — logged as the work happens, not reconstructed at tax time — and courts have consistently ruled against taxpayers who estimated hours after the fact. Most owners fail to qualify not because they didn't do the work, but because they can't prove it in a form that survives an audit.

    Core insight The product being sold isn't a stopwatch. It's audit defensibility. Every design decision should be judged against: does this make the record more contemporaneous, more complete, and more defensible?

    2. Market Context

    Four direct competitors are live today: REPSLog, HourProof, STR Loophole (strhours.com), and Field Ledger. All four predate the current generation of agentic AI tooling — their "AI" features are bolt-on autofill or rule-based text parsing layered onto a conventional form-and-timer app, not an agent-first architecture.

    2.2 Key takeaways

    • STR Loophole is the competitor to watch — free tier, a tax-savings-calculator lead magnet, and a multi-product suite (REPS Time, Kids Payroll) signal a funded operator with an active growth motion.
    • Passive capture from booking platforms is no longer a gap — STR Loophole already syncs PMS and calendar data. Differentiation now requires going a layer deeper: location, bank transactions, smart-lock access, and photo evidence, fused together.
    • NL parsing exists but is weak — Field Ledger's parser is explicitly rule-based. LLM-grade extraction with clarifying questions and personalization is still an execution gap, not a concept gap.
    • Documentation scope is expanding beyond hours — Field Ledger's trip/mileage and expense logging is a good signal: a complete audit package beats a pure hour log.
    • Still wide open across all four: real-time contemporaneity/backfill-risk scoring, an interactive legal-grounding copilot, proactive household constraint-checking, and any wearable/SMS/offline-first capture.

    3. Product Vision & Positioning

    Positioning statement: the only material participation tracker that fuses passive signals beyond bookings — location, bank activity, smart locks, photos — into an AI-parsed, contemporaneity-scored audit defense package, captured hands-free anywhere, backed by a legal-grounding copilot that competitors only reference in marketing copy.

    The brand promise is retention, not features: the app that's still being used in November, because logging requires near-zero effort, not because it has the longest feature list.

    4. Target Users

    • Primary: Self-managed STR owner (1–3 properties), W-2 income, pursuing the 100-hour or 500-hour material participation test to offset active income with rental losses.
    • Secondary: Multi-property STR portfolio operator, working with co-hosts, cleaners, and property managers, needs per-property and per-person tracking plus the "no one else's time exceeds yours" constraint managed automatically.
    • Secondary: Real estate professional pursuing REPS (750-hour test, 50%+ of working time in real estate), typically a longer-term-rental-heavy or mixed portfolio.
    • Influencer stakeholder, not a direct user: CPAs & cost segregation firms who recommend a tool to clients and receive the year-end export. Their trust is a distribution channel (see Section 9).

    5. Design Principles

    1. AI is the primary surface, manual is a first-class backup, not an afterthought Every AI-drafted entry and every manually-typed entry produces the identical structured record. AI drafts land in a pending state for one-tap confirm or edit — that review step is the manual backup, not a separate mode. A full manual "Add Entry" form is always one tap away, never buried behind chat.
    2. Capture must work without a screen A meaningful share of material participation work happens hands-dirty, away from any device. The primary capture surfaces are zero-touch (geofence, calendar, PMS signals), one-touch (watch complication, quick-settings tile, SMS to a dedicated number, a photo), and only then a full app screen for detailed review — reserved for later, comfortable moments, not the work moment itself.
    3. Trust and rigor apply equally regardless of input path Contemporaneity timestamping (created_at vs. activity_date) applies to every entry, AI or manual. The product's core deliverable is a record that survives an audit, so no capture path gets to be less rigorous for the sake of speed.

    6. Feature Scope (v0)

    6.1 Capture layer

    • Natural-language logging via text, voice memo, or forwarded SMS/iMessage — no app open required
    • Photo-first logging: a timestamped, geotagged photo alone can become a drafted entry
    • Passive signal drafts: geofence presence, calendar events, PMS/booking data (turnovers, guest messaging windows) generate pending entries automatically
    • Bank/Plaid transaction signals (e.g., a hardware store charge) suggest a task and prompt for hours
    • Offline-first capture with local buffering and background sync (rural properties, poor connectivity)

    6.2 AI copilot & audit defense

    • Agent-native entry extraction: property, task, duration, participant, and category parsed from natural language, with a single clarifying question only when needed
    • Contemporaneity scoring per entry (flags entries created long after the activity date as backfill risk)
    • Vague-entry detection ("worked on house" prompts for specificity)
    • Unrealistic-duration flagging (e.g., 18 logged hours in one day)
    • Interactive legal-grounding chat: "would this count?" answered against the actual material participation tests and known case outcomes
    • Test-fit recommendation: suggests which of the material participation tests the user is actually on track for, and flags when a chosen test is fragile

    6.3 Household & team

    • Multi-participant logging (spouse, cleaner, contractor, co-host, property manager) via shared link or SMS, no app install required for non-primary participants
    • Real-time constraint checking for the 100-hour test: warns proactively if another participant's logged time risks exceeding the primary user's
    • Per-property and per-person breakdowns

    6.4 Manual & trust layer

    • Full manual entry form, always one tap from anywhere in the app
    • Every field on every entry editable after the fact, regardless of capture method
    • Quick-add templates for repeat tasks (e.g., "weekly turnover clean") as one-tap shortcuts
    • Correction feedback loop: edits to AI drafts personalize future parsing for that user's vocabulary and property names

    6.5 Reporting & export

    • Year-end CPA-ready package: narrative summary, timeline, attachments, and a confidence/readiness score with specific flagged gaps
    • Export by property, category, or date range
    • Pacing dashboard: progress toward the selected test's hour threshold, with a projection given current logging rate

    6.6 Platform & portability

    • iOS and Android apps, feature parity between the two
    • Wear OS and Apple Watch companions for one-touch voice capture, no phone required in hand
    • Android quick-settings tile and iOS lock-screen/Action Button equivalents as the primary hands-free trigger
    • Web app scoped to "back office" functions — CPA export, bulk review, settings — not day-to-day capture

    6.7 Live progress & quick capture

    Two glanceable surfaces, one on each platform, keep year-to-date progress visible without opening the app, and turn the same surface into a one-tap entry point back into capture.

    • iOS — Live Activity (ActivityKit): shown in the Dynamic Island and on the Lock Screen while a capture session is active, displaying the running session timer alongside year-to-date progress toward the selected test threshold (e.g., "62/100 hrs"). Tapping it deep-links straight into quick capture or stop-and-confirm.
    • Android — Live Update (Android 16+, Notification.ProgressStyle): the direct equivalent, surfaced as a status bar chip, in the notification shade, on the lock screen, and on the Always-On Display on supported devices — same running-timer-plus-year-progress content as iOS.
    • Always-on fallback: Live Activities/Live Updates are scoped to active, bounded sessions by design, not indefinite background display. A persistent Home Screen/Lock Screen widget (WidgetKit on iOS, App Widget on Android) carries the same year-to-date progress at all other times, so it's visible whether or not a session is currently running.
    • The Live Activity/Live Update, the widget, and the quick-settings tile/Action Button (Section 6.6) all route to the same one-touch capture entry point — one consistent "tap here to log" affordance across every glanceable surface, reinforcing the no-unlock, no-home-screen capture principle.

    7. Out of Scope for v0

    • Native Assistant/Gemini voice routing via platform agent APIs (Android AppFunctions is in private preview as of mid-2026; revisit once generally available)
    • Full bookkeeping/accounting functionality — this is a documentation system, not a ledger replacement
    • CPA/firm-side portal or white-label offering (candidate for the partnership phase, see Section 9)
    • Non-STR/REPS material participation use cases outside real estate

    8. AI-Native Architecture Principles

    Competitors built a form first and added an AI autofill button second. This product should be architected the opposite way: the agent is the primary interface, and structured tools are what it calls.

    • Agent with tools, not one-shot extraction — log_entry, split_time, flag_risk, query_case_law, and check_household_constraint are callable functions the same agent uses across every surface (chat, voice, SMS), rather than separate one-off features.
    • Multimodal input in a single reasoning pass — voice, photo, and text handled together rather than as separate pipelines.
    • RAG over IRS regulations and Tax Court outcomes powers the legal-grounding copilot; this is now cheap infrastructure, not a research investment.
    • Server-side agent logic so behavior changes ship instantly, without an app-store review cycle — a real iteration-speed advantage over competitors' in-app parsing logic.
    • Correction-as-training-signal pipeline built in from day one — every user edit to an AI draft is captured as structured feedback that improves that user's future parsing, not just logged and discarded.
    • Reliability over cleverness on the legally-consequential path — structured outputs, validation, and a human-confirm step matter more here than open-ended agentic flexibility.
    • Built agent-callable from day one — positioned to plug into platform-level agent routing (Android AppFunctions, Apple App Intents) as those mature, rather than retrofitting a legacy form-based app later.
    Caveat Being AI-native is an execution multiplier, not a standalone moat — a funded competitor (STR Loophole in particular) can bolt on better AI too. The durable moat is the combination of the correction-driven data flywheel, integration depth, and CPA/firm distribution trust described in Section 9; AI-native architecture is what makes building those faster and cheaper.

    9. Go-to-Market Strategy

    9.1 Trust moat

    • A real CPA or real-estate tax attorney as advisor, named publicly
    • Content grounded in actual Tax Court outcomes and IRS guidance, doubling as SEO and as the source material for the in-app legal copilot
    • A premium "audit defense" tier: CPA-ready package generation plus concierge support if a user is actually audited — reframes the product from time tracker to audit insurance, supporting pricing above the market's freemium floor

    9.2 Distribution

    • CPA / cost-segregation firm referral or partner program — B2B2C is the primary growth lever in this niche, more than app-store discovery
    • Direct outreach to the small set of STR tax-strategy creators who already drive this audience's education (YouTube/Instagram/TikTok)
    • SEO content competing directly for "material participation," "STR loophole hours," and "REPS tracking" queries currently owned by STR Loophole and Field Ledger
    • Presence in BiggerPockets and STR-specific community forums/Discords/Facebook groups

    9.3 Retention focus

    Churn — not signups — is the real risk: users start motivated in January and stop logging by spring, leaving unusable data by tax time. Passive capture attacks this structurally; marketing and product nudges should concentrate on two windows: January (New Year tax-planning mindset) and Q4 (year-end deadline pressure), rather than flat spend year-round.

    9.4 Pricing

    Validate against Field Ledger's $19–79/mo range rather than assume unlimited headroom — the market has already tested premium pricing here. A lean, AI-native cost structure supports either undercutting the freemium floor or reinvesting margin into the CPA distribution motion instead of requiring a raise to fund it.

    10. Success Metrics (draft, to refine at MVP scoping)

    • % of users still logging weekly in month 9+ of their first tax year (the core retention/churn metric)
    • Median time from activity to log entry (contemporaneity — lower is better)
    • % of entries created via zero-touch/one-touch paths vs. full manual form
    • % of users reaching their chosen test's hour threshold by year end
    • CPA/firm-referred signups as a share of total signups

    11. Risks & Open Questions

    Competitive risk STR Loophole appears funded, multi-product, and already has the deepest integrations in market. If they add passive signal fusion or a legal copilot before this product ships, the wedge narrows. Speed to MVP on the differentiated pieces (contemporaneity scoring, legal copilot, wearable capture) matters.
    Privacy sensitivity Location, bank transaction, and smart-lock signals are high-sensitivity data categories. Passive capture needs clear opt-in, on-device triage where possible, and a privacy posture that itself becomes a trust signal rather than a liability.
    • Open: build vs. partner for PMS integrations (Hospitable, Airbnb, VRBO, Booking.com) — how fast can parity with STR Loophole's integration depth be reached?
    • Open: is a CPA/firm partner program viable pre-launch, or does it require a proven product first?
    • Open: exact MVP platform sequencing — iOS + Android simultaneously, or one first with Wear OS/Watch as fast-follow?
    • Open: pricing model — flat subscription vs. tiered by property count vs. value-based pricing anchored to estimated tax savings.
