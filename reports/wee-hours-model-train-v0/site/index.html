Teaching Laya what a tired parent means

Stock Laya picks the right category for a voice log 37% of the time, so the app almost never logs without asking. A fine-tune on ~12,700 synthetic, spoken-then-transcribed sentences takes it to 98% (type 99.4%) and lets the app log 88% of sentences on its own, with one wrong entry in 178, at the same speed and size.

1Summary

What worked

  • Labels decided first, teacher only phrases them, blind relabel filters: 60% of what the teacher wrote survived, and the survivors are clean.
  • Real speech errors: Kokoro reads every sentence aloud in a noisy room, Whisper base.en (the app's model, with its prompt) transcribes it. More than half the transcripts differ from the text.
  • Training on the app's request verbatim means no prompt drift: the GGUF drops into the app's existing runtime.
  • Targeted data works: diagnosing why one class was starved (190 → 1,195 rows) fixed both past-bottle errors and the “wash the bottles” one in a single 1.5 h run.
  • The whole run fits in an afternoon on the DGX Spark: 1 h 41 min teacher, 40 min training.

What's left

  • One wrong auto-commit left: “remember to buy more formula” logged as a bottle. “Not a feed” sentences that mention feeding things are the next weak class.
  • Side detection softened (“switching sides, now left” goes to pick-two; side accuracy 100 → 90%): not logged wrong, but an extra tap.
  • The eval is 178 hand-written sentences, all typed text: no real audio, no real parents yet.
  • Calibration temperatures are fitted on 89 rows; treat calibrated numbers as rough.

Recommendation: filled in when the top-up finishes.

Update, 2026-10-19. The top-up shipped in 0.4.0. A second round fixed “not a log entry” and side detection and ships in 0.4.1 (§14); a third round teaches the model the 13 new care, health and activity types (§15).

2What Laya does in the app

Laya (convaiinnovations/laya, ModernBERT-large, 421M parameters) is not a chatbot: it scores options. The app asks it multiple-choice questions about the transcript and does everything else (numbers, clock times, names, sessions) deterministically.

  • Hierarchical questions (Interpreter.swift): “What is the sentence about?” (category), then “starting, switching, finishing, or a complete entry?” (phase), then the type's fields (amount, side, contents, diaper kind, solids amount). Each question is rendered as [CLS]choice question: …[SEP][MASK] key: description…[SEP]{transcript}[SEP] (LayaPrompt.swift).
  • The descriptions in spec/event-types.json are part of the prompt. Changing a word there changes what the model sees, for stock and fine-tuned alike. That turned out to matter (§9).
  • Laya sees only the transcript. Whether a bottle is open is applied afterwards as priors (e.g. finish ×1.6 when a bottle is open, ×0.9 when not), so the model has to get past-tense one-entry bottles right from the words.
  • Confidence policy: the weakest question's probability decides the tier. Auto-commit logs it, pick-two shows two choices, full list shows everything. A wrong auto-commit is the costly error: the parent has to notice and undo it.

6Training

What kind of training this is

Supervised fine-tuning of a classifier on distilled labels, not teacher forcing. Teacher forcing applies to autoregressive generators (feed the gold prefix, predict the next token). Laya generates nothing: it's an encoder that reads [CLS]choice question: …[SEP][MASK] start: …[MASK] finish: …[SEP]{transcript}[SEP] in one pass and scores each option at its [MASK]; a softmax over those scores is the answer.

  • Distillation through data only, hard labels. gpt-oss-120b writes the sentences and its blind answers decide which survive, but the target is the plan's label, fixed before the teacher wrote anything. No teacher probabilities reach the student.
  • One-hot targets per question. Each row becomes one item per question it answers (category, phase, fields): 10,958 rows → 22,286 items.
  • The loss (rlcd, the trainer's default) = soft cross-entropy over the question's own options (the SFT part) + a GRPO-style policy-gradient term: Gaussian noise added to the detached option scores, 4 samples, each rewarded by a strictly proper scoring rule (log + spherical score), noise annealed over the epochs. That term targets calibrated probabilities, not accuracy, and makes per-step losses swing and dip below zero. The trainer's own notes (#741) found no gain from it over soft-CE alone (§11).
  • Option order is fixed (shuffling is off by default), matching the app, which always presents options in spec order.

Setup

  • Base convaiinnovations/laya, laya.train.finetune (RLCD)
  • 4 epochs, micro-batch 8 × grad-accum 8, encoder LR 2.5e-5, head LR 1e-4
  • 22,286 question items (rows × the questions each answers), 1,396 optimizer updates
  • 400 calibration items; 1,310 held-out synthetic items
  • ~40 min on the Spark GPU; convert to Q8_0 GGUF (449 MB, same as stock) in 2 min

14Round 2: “not a log entry” (shipped in 0.4.1)

The weakness listed for the top-up, “11 of 12 not-a-log sentences open the full list”, was mostly the app, not the model.

What the diagnosis found

  • Interpreter.swift sent every “none of these” answer to the full list on purpose, and logeval's review file then showed the first listed entry as “got”. So “hiccups again → Diaper” meant the model had said none.
  • With the model's own answer recorded (new model_category, none_confidence in the review), the top-up said “none” for 32 of 42 not-a-log sentences, mostly at 89–97%.
  • The 10 real misses were near misses that name a log topic without reporting one: “we need more nappies” → diaper 94%, “remember to buy more formula” → bottle, “booked the six week check” → growth. The category question literally asked “What is the parent's sentence about?”.
  • The eval had only 12 not-a-log sentences, so one sentence was 8% of the class.

What changed

  • App: a confident “not a log entry” (at the 85% auto bar) is kept as a note without asking, with a “Log it” toast. The keyword fallback keeps “none” below the bar: without that it would have turned 11.5% of real entries into notes.
  • Wording: “Which kind of baby log entry is the parent reporting?”, and the none option names errands, plans, questions, chores, health and milestones.
  • Eval: 30 hand-written not-a-log sentences (208 rows). The generator now carries the earlier bottle relabels, which had only been made in the generated file.
  • Data: configs/none.toml, 1,000 plans, 60% not-a-log over 12 new near-miss topics plus short fragments, with real entries of the same topics for contrast; trained with full + top-up (14,490 rows). Teacher 10 min, training 57 min.
  • Infrastructure: every run's text data is on Hugging Face (wee-hours-baby-activity-data, tags = run names; publish_data, fetch), and the app pins its model to a tag (LAYA_REVISION), so publishing a newer run never changes what a released version downloads.

15Round 3: care, health and activity types

0.4.1 adds 13 tap-logged trackers (bath, lotion, tooth brushing, potty, teething, medication, symptoms, temperature, doctor visits, vaccinations, tummy time, play time, walks). In the app they are "model": false: never offered to the model, so its prompt is byte-for-byte what it was trained on. This round, on branch weehours-laya-care, teaches it them.

  • Design: a “care” option and a care_type question. Laya caps a question and all its option descriptions at 192 tokens (head_max_len), so 21 category options won't fit. The category question gets one “care” option (9 options, ~112 words) and a care_type question picks among the enabled trackers (13 options, ~106 words); P(type) = P(care) × P(care_type), the same shape as category × phase.
  • Eval (239 rows): 31 new care sentences, and 12 sentences that were “not a log entry” relabelled as entries now that the types exist. These are my calls, for your review: rash, cough, sneezing, goopy eyes, baby acne → symptom; “gave her the vitamin D drops” → medication; “tummy time on the play mat” → tummy time; “she's teething I think” → teething; “temperature is 37.2” → temperature; “pediatrician says she's doing great” → doctor visit; “we're going for a walk” → walk; “the bath water was too cold” → bath. Plans and questions stay not-a-log (“bath at seven tonight”, “is she due for her vaccines soon”, “booked the six week check”), and so do hiccups and nail clipping.
  • Data: configs/care.toml, 1,500 plans: 65% the 13 types, 15% not-a-log near misses (planning a bath, asking about a medicine or vaccine), 20% core entries; trained with full + top-up + none. The earlier runs' not-a-log rows about topics that are now entries (bath, drops, tummy time…) are wrong labels today, so build drops them (drop_none_matching).
  • Keyword fallback: a care lexicon keeps CI's keyword gate green (type 93.3%, wrong auto-commits 1.0%); “ointment” had to become “ ointment” (it matched “appointment”).
  • Not done in the app yet: voice for types with required fields (medicine name, temperature value, symptom, potty result) needs a voice path for those fields; the branch only trains and measures the type.

Run: 1,500 plans, teacher 27 min (blind agreement 0.629), TTS 15 min, training 68 min on 16,838 rows (135–245 per new type); 916 stale not-a-log rows dropped.

16Round 4: two-level questions, retrained from base

Folding 13 categories into one “care” option grouped by the token cap, not by meaning (a bath, a fever and a walk side by side). The redesign (branch weehours-laya-groups) asks two levels: a group (feeding, diaper, sleep, growth, health, care, activity, none of these), then, for a group with several enabled categories, a <group>_entry question (e.g. health: medication, symptom, temperature, teething, doctor, vaccination). Sessions keep the phase question. Every question has at most 8 options.

  • No new teacher data: build now recomputes every row's labels from its type and fields (the port of logeval's gold()), so full + top-up + none + care are relabelled for the new questions and the model is trained from base on all of it.
  • Categories turned off: the app only offers enabled categories, but every training row and the eval used all of them. Half the training rows are now asked with a random subset (the Python cut is tested against the app's own default-household export), and logeval --categories default scores a new household (187 rows).
  • Voice for the new types: required fields come from the words (medicine name = the sentence, symptom and potty result by keyword or “other”, temperature from the number, °F above 45); if one can't be filled the entry is asked about, never logged incomplete.
  • Near-miss top-up: a 700-plan nearmiss run (plans and questions about baths, medicine, vaccines, doctors, walks) feeds a second model, groups2, queued after groups.
