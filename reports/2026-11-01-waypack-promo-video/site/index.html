Waypack promo video kit (Google Flow)A 30 s winter road-trip comedy: zero bars, and Rosa doesn't care · story, characters, Flow prompts, music prompts

Up in the mountains, there's no signal. Her trip doesn't need one.

The plan for Waypack's promo: a 30-second comedy plus 15-second cuts in 16:9 and 9:16, set on a winter family trip among giant snowy trees, in the spirit of the app's own example, "3 days in Sequoia over Christmas with a toddler". Rosa's AI agent planned the trip weeks ago, and she tapped Download at home. Now there are zero bars, and she has what's next, an offline map with her GPS dot, the cabin's check-in and the backup plan. Her running gag is her brother Gus, who was going to "just look it up when we get there" and spends the trip hunting for one bar of signal. You make the characters, the shots and the music in Google Flow from the prompts below; I make the screen recordings of the app and the live preview, the voiceover and the edit.

Grounded in the app

  • Every claim comes from projects/waypack (site, store listing, design): any MCP agent plans the trip "with Waypack", researches, geocodes and computes real routes, sends a live preview that fills in as it plans, and publishes a trip guide; the app downloads it while there's signal and then works in airplane mode: Today ("what's happening now and next"), day-by-day plans with the why, lodging and check-in, backup plans, and an offline map with trails, routes and the GPS dot.
  • The offline map is Pro (#54/#55), so the end card says so; the app itself never sells anything (#25), and the video doesn't show a price or a purchase.
  • Colours from the logo: terracotta on cream, with the map's warm paper and brown ink.

Built in from the last videos

  • Fast: a cut every 2–4 beats of the music, each Flow shot used for 1.5–3 s.
  • No green screens: every phone's back faces the camera (or it's in a car mount angled away) and the laptop's lid faces the camera. The product is shown in real recordings.
  • Not another commute: Headway and G2 Digest are trains; this is a snowy mountain road trip in daylight and warm cabin light, so the channel's videos don't look alike.

The story: zero bars, and she doesn't care

A snowy pullout high in the mountains, giant red-barked trees, a family car with skis on the roof. Gus, who planned nothing ("I'll just look it up when we get there"), wanders in circles in the snow, holding his phone up to the sky. "Does anyone have a single bar?" Rosa, her toddler Pip on her hip, turns to the camera: "Don't need one." A glance at her phone: Today says what's now and what's next. In the car on the snowy road, the offline map shows their route and their blue dot. At dusk, at the cabin, she reads the check-in details off her phone and opens the lockbox while Gus holds his phone up to the porch light. Flashback: weeks before, on the couch at home, she asked her agent to plan it, watched the trip take shape, and tapped Download on the Wi-Fi. Payoff: a sledding hill, Rosa and Pip flying past laughing; Gus, phone finally in his pocket, turns to the camera: "Okay. Where's the sledding hill?"

The characters: Rosa, and Gus

The same movie style as the Headway, FitLoop and G2 Digest promos, so the channel feels like one family: photoreal, expressive faces in cinematic light, a lead who plays to the camera, a comic foil who never does (until his last line). Both are new, original characters. Pip, the toddler, is written into the shot prompts only (no lines, no character card): Flow keeps a child consistent enough across a few shots, and the edit uses Pip for a second at a time.

What to do, in order

  1. Settle the story: notes on the story and characters, then Copy summary and paste it back.
  2. Make Rosa and Gus in Flow (portrait, Create body, character info, voice); keep the ones you like.
  3. Make shot 2 first (both characters, both lines, Pip on her hip), then the other six. Keep takes with no logos, signs or badges and nothing readable on screens.
  4. Make the music in Flow Music, both lengths.
  5. Drop the files into branding/promo-videos/public/waypack-launch/, any names, saying which is which.
  6. Ask for the edit. Done: the recordings, the voiceover and the three cuts are in §1, with the YouTube kit in §9.

What can go wrong

ProblemWhat to do
Flow draws park signs, a park-service logo or uniform, car badges or a licence plateRegenerate with "no signs, no logos, no badges, no plates" again at the end, or use a take where it's out of focus or cropped.
A phone or the laptop turns toward the camera and shows a screenUse the part before it does, or regenerate; every prompt says the screens face away.
Shot 4 looks like distracted driving (Rosa looking at the phone)Use only takes where her eyes stay on the road; regenerate with "eyes on the road the whole time". If Flow insists, park the car and keep the phone in the mount.
Rosa and Gus blend into each other (siblings, similar colouring)Make shot 2 first as the test. His red trapper hat and Hawaiian shirt keep them apart; if Flow still mixes them, make his part as its own shot and the edit cuts between them.
Pip changes between shots, or a child looks uncannyKeep Pip small in frame and use him for a second at a time; if a take looks wrong, cut to Rosa alone.
Gus's falls look painfulKeep takes where he lands softly in snow and gives the thumbs-up; the joke is his optimism.
Flow ad-libs extra linesThe edit keeps only the written lines and mutes the rest; I transcribe every take first.
Answered: the device-map download recorded in one take (≈7 s, sped up in the edit), and Flow kept Rosa, Gus and Pip apart in shot 2. Still open: how the mix sounds, which only your ears can tell.
