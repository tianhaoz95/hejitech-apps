    Pricing & Tier Proposal

    A market study of the four direct competitors, a re-check of their live pricing as of July 2026, and a permanent two-tier structure — one free, one paid — priced below the category's premium anchor because on-device AI genuinely makes this product cheaper to run than any competitor's cloud-only pipeline.

    Status: Draft — for decision Version: v0 Date: July 31, 2026 Owner: Jackson Zhou Scope: Monetization strategy, pre-Stripe-integration

    1. Recommendation, up front

    Bottom line Two tiers, permanently — not a launch-phase stopgap: Free (1 property, full capture, core dashboard, CSV export) and a single Pro tier at $9.99/mo or $99/yr (unlimited properties, the legal copilot, CPA-ready export). No third tier is planned even once household/team logging ships — those features fold into the same Pro price rather than spawning a new SKU. The price sits below Field Ledger's $19 anchor on purpose: on-device parsing and deterministic risk-flagging cost Contempo close to nothing per user, unlike competitors who likely run every entry through a cloud LLM call. That's a real structural cost advantage, not just a growth-hack discount, so it's sustainable rather than a promotional price that has to climb back up later.

    2. Market landscape (re-verified July 2026)

    reports/prd-v0.html §2.1 and §12 already captured solid competitor research. Re-checking each competitor's live pricing today turned up one correction and one real market shift worth acting on:

    Product Current pricing Change vs. original PRD research
    Field Ledger Owner $19/mo ($190/yr) · Operator $39/mo ($390/yr) · Portfolio $79/mo ($790/yr); 7-day free trial, no card required Unchanged. The PRD's "714-day free trial" was a scrape error — it's 7 days. This is the most stable, most direct comparable in the category; treat it as the pricing anchor.
    HourProof Pro $9.99/mo ($49.99/yr) · Premium ~$12.99/mo (~$99.99/yr) Premium looks to have come down from $14.99/$149.99 — a competitor moving pricing down, another reason not to chase the low end.
    REPSLog Free download, monetized via in-app purchase (exact IAP price not published) Real shift. Originally a flat $149.99– 199.99/yr purchase; now free-to-download with paid unlock. The whole category is drifting toward "free app, paid depth" as the default shape, not flat annual licenses.
    STR Loophole family (strhours.com, plus newer sibling apps: STR Tax Loophole Tracker, REPS Time) Free with in-app purchase, ~30-day free trial Confirms the free-tier/freemium floor is real and multi-app — this operator now runs several apps on the same free-download model rather than one flat-priced product.

    Net read: the market has two clusters — a $9.99–15/mo commodity band (HourProof, and REPSLog/STR Loophole's paid unlocks likely land here) and a $19–79/mo professional band occupied almost solely by Field Ledger. Nobody in the commodity band has Field Ledger's feature depth (unlimited properties, a real copilot) — landing Contempo Pro at the top of the commodity band instead of at Field Ledger's price lets it undercut the one competitor with comparable depth while still out-featuring everyone at its own price point.

    3. Why Contempo can price below Field Ledger and still hold margin

    This only works because the AI cost structure isn't uniform across features — it's concentrated in exactly one place:

    Capability Marginal cost per use Why
    Entry parsing / transcription ~$0 Runs on-device via Firebase AI Logic's hybrid on-device/cloud path (android/agent/AgentClient.kt) — no cloud inference call for the common case
    Contemporaneity/vagueness risk scoring (flagRisk) $0 Deterministic rules, not an LLM call at all
    Legal-grounding copilot (askAgent) Real, recurring The one function that always calls cloud Gemini Flash — already cost-gated centrally via the cloud_ai_enabled Remote Config kill switch

    Competitors almost certainly route every AI-assisted entry through a cloud model call — HourProof's "AI-assisted logging," REPSLog's "AI autofill," STR Loophole's parsing all read as cloud-side features in their marketing copy, with no on-device story. Contempo pays that cloud-cost tax on exactly one feature (the copilot), not on every entry a user logs. That's the structural reason $9.99/mo is sustainable here in a way it might not be for a cloud-only competitor — it's not a subsidized growth-hack price.

    3.1 The AI story doesn't cover the whole bill — storage is a separate, compounding cost

    A real gap in the reasoning above On-device inference and a deterministic risk-flagger make the compute side of Free genuinely near-zero. But every entry — even a plain text one — is a Firestore document, and every photo or odometer capture is a Cloud Storage object, and neither of those costs anything to do with AI. Unlike the copilot's cloud-LLM cost, which is a bounded, per-request charge, storage cost has a different shape: it accumulates forever per user, because §4 already rules out a time-limited trial or any data expiry on Free — that was the right call for trust, but it means a Free user's storage footprint only ever grows, year over year, with no reset. At small scale this is genuinely cheap (Firestore is roughly $0.18/100k reads or writes and ~$0.026/GiB-month; Cloud Storage is similar per-GB) — a single free user logging text entries for years costs fractions of a cent. But it compounds across every free account ever created, indefinitely, which is a different risk profile than a per-transaction AI cost that resets with usage.

    The fix isn't to meter entry count or add retention limits — both would recreate the exact trust problem §4 already argues against (a host mid-tax-year suddenly finding old records inaccessible or new ones blocked). The actual cost driver is attachment bytes, not entry count: a timestamped text entry is a few hundred bytes in Firestore regardless of volume, while a photo or odometer image is one to several megabytes in Cloud Storage. So the lever should be on photo/attachment volume specifically, not on how many hours or entries a Free user logs:

    • Keep entries themselves fully unlimited on Free — manual, voice, timers, mileage trips, all of it, indefinitely. This is what makes the free tier a real product and not a crippled demo, and it costs Contempo almost nothing regardless of volume.
    • Meter stored photo/odometer attachments on Free — e.g., a capped number of stored images (illustratively ~50–100 lifetime, or per year) or a storage-size cap (e.g., ~250MB), with Pro removing the cap. The exact number needs real usage data before it's fixed (see §8) — this proposal sets the mechanism, not the final number.
    • Compress/downsample images on upload for every tier, not just Free — this shrinks the baseline liability across the whole user base and is good practice independent of pricing.

    One more mitigating factor worth naming: Free is already capped at 1 property, which caps the worst-case blast radius somewhat — a Free user can only accumulate one property's worth of photos, not an unlimited portfolio's. Multi-property users who'd actually generate meaningful storage volume are already paying for Pro.

    3.2 A bigger gap than storage: entry parsing isn't guaranteed to be free either

    This one undercuts §3's core argument, not just adds a footnote to it §3 treated "entry parsing / transcription: ~$0" as a flat fact. It isn't — it's the default behavior, not a guarantee. AgentClient.kt reads entry parsing through a user-controlled InferencePreference (ON_DEVICE_ONLY / PREFER_ON_DEVICE / CLOUD_ONLY, exposed directly in Settings, default PREFER_ON_DEVICE). Two real cost leaks follow from that:
    • Silent cloud fallback on the default setting — PREFER_ON_DEVICE tries the on-device model (Gemma via LiteRT-LM) and falls back to a cloud Genkit call whenever that model is unavailable on the device. On-device inference needs specific hardware/OS support, so an unknown but nonzero share of free users — plausibly skewed toward older/budget devices, which are also the price-sensitive users most likely to pick Free — will silently hit the cloud path on every single entry, not occasionally.
    • No entitlement gating exists on this at all — SettingsScreen.kt lets any user, on any tier, select CLOUD_ONLY today, which routes every parse and every copilot question to cloud Gemini with no usage cap or tier check anywhere in the code. That's expected pre-Stripe (there's no entitlement system yet to check against) but it means the "copilot is Pro-only, parsing is basically free" split this proposal relies on isn't actually enforced anywhere yet.

    Net effect: unlike photo storage (small, predictable, just needs a quota), this is a genuine unbounded liability on Free today — a Free user in CLOUD_ONLY mode, or simply on a device without on-device model support, logging entries daily for a year, generates real recurring cloud-LLM cost with nothing currently stopping it. This needs to be closed before Free launches at scale, not just monitored: recommend that once entitlement checking exists, CLOUD_ONLY requires Pro, and PREFER_ON_DEVICE's cloud-fallback path on Free carries its own modest monthly quota (graceful degradation to "try again on Wi-Fi/a newer device, or enter manually" past the quota, not a hard failure that loses the entry) — the same honesty-over-silent-fallback principle AgentClient.kt's own code comments already apply to ON_DEVICE_ONLY mode.

    4. Other pricing principles worth stating explicitly

    Reject: pricing tied to estimated tax savings The PRD (§11) left "value-based pricing anchored to estimated tax savings" as an open question. Recommend closing it as no. Computing "how much we saved you" requires the user's full return (income, marginal rate, passive-loss carryovers) — data this product doesn't and shouldn't collect — and billing a percentage of a tax outcome reads as tax advice, which is a materially different (and riskier) business than a documentation tool. STR Loophole's savings calculator is the right pattern: use it as marketing/onboarding framing ("hosts like you typically document $X in deductions"), never as the billing mechanism. Flat subscription stays.
    A second, less obvious call: no time-limited trial Every competitor here (Field Ledger, STR Loophole, REPSLog) sells on a free trial that expires. That's a bad fit for this specific product's promise: the entire pitch is continuous, contemporaneous records that survive an audit years later. A trial that lapses mid-tax-year and locks a host out of their own log — right when the IRS test cares about unbroken documentation — undermines the trust story this product is selling. A metered free tier (capped by property count, not by time) never puts a user's existing records at risk and costs nothing to keep running. Recommend leading with metering over trials, unlike every direct competitor.

    5. The proposed tier — one free, one paid, permanently

    No phasing, no third SKU. When household/team logging or passive signal sync eventually ship, they land inside the same Pro price — the whole point of collapsing to one paid tier is that it stays true regardless of what gets built next, not just at launch.

    Tier Price Includes
    Free $0 1 property · unlimited manual/voice entries, timers & mileage trips (with pause/resume) · photo/odometer capture up to a metered cap (illustratively ~50–100 stored images; exact number TBD, see §8) · participant-role tagging for the 100-/500-hour tests · core hours-vs-threshold dashboard with inline risk flags · CSV export
    Pro $9.99/mo or $99/yr Everything in Free, plus: unlimited properties · unlimited photo/odometer storage (removes the Free attachment cap) · the legal-grounding copilot (freeform Q&A against real IRS/Tax Court guidance — the one feature with real cloud cost) · a polished CPA-ready export package (formatted PDF with risk annotations, not just raw CSV) · Wear OS / watchOS capture and complications · whatever ships later — household/team logging, PMS/calendar sync, geofencing all fold in here rather than becoming a new tier

    The pitch this makes possible: more than Field Ledger's $19/mo Owner tier (which caps at 1 property, 2 users, no AI) for about half the price, and more capability than HourProof or REPSLog's paid unlocks at roughly the same price those charge today. That combination — beat the premium competitor on price, beat the budget competitors on features — is only credible because of the cost structure in §3; without the on-device-parsing advantage this would just be underpricing.

    Household/team logging isn't built yet — that doesn't block this pricing decision Per reports/prd-status-v0.html §5, real-time household constraint checking and multi-participant accounts are explicitly not started, and the Firestore data model is still single-owner only. That's fine under a single-tier structure: it just means Pro subscribers get a feature added to their existing plan once it ships, with no new pricing decision required at that point. The failure mode this avoids is the one from the three-tier version of this proposal — a "Portfolio" tier priced around a feature that doesn't exist yet.

    5.1 Concierge audit-defense: an add-on, not a tier feature

    The PRD (§9.1) floats a concierge audit-defense package — human CPA review and support if a user is actually audited — as a premium differentiator. Recommend keeping this decoupled from Pro entirely: it's a staffed service with real marginal cost per claim, not software, and doesn't fit "on-device AI keeps costs low" the way everything else in Pro does. Sell it as a standalone annual add-on (illustratively ~$99–199/yr) once there's a paying base large enough to justify the operational lift of actually staffing it — bundling an unstaffed service into the subscription price is a promise the business can't yet keep.

    7. What this changes vs. today's placeholder

    The landing page and web/src/pages/BillingPage currently show a Starter/Pro/Portfolio mockup at $0/$19/$49. This proposal replaces it with a two-card layout and a lower Pro price:

    • Drop the Portfolio card entirely rather than mark it "coming soon" — a single visible paid tier is the point, not a temporary launch state.
    • Change Pro's price from $19/mo to $9.99/mo ($99/yr) and its copy to lead with the on-device/cost-advantage framing from §3, not just a feature list — the lower price needs the "why" or it just reads as a discount.
    • Reassign "unlimited properties" from an unqualified Pro perk to one that needs a small enforcement change first (property-count limit isn't currently enforced anywhere for the Free tier) — flagged in §6 as build work, not just a pricing decision.
    • Stop advertising Free's photo/odometer capture as unqualified "unlimited" — per §3.1, entries stay unlimited but attachment storage should carry a metered cap, with Pro removing it.
    • Stop describing entry parsing as simply "free/on-device" in any marketing copy — per §3.2 it's only the default, not a guarantee, until entitlement gating actually exists.

    8. Open items before this can go live

    • Wire Stripe via the Firebase Extension (web/src/billing/subscription.ts TODO) — the actual checkout/webhook/entitlement-sync work.
    • Add a property-count limit to firestore.rules and a client-side gate for Free-tier accounts at 1 property.
    • Instrument actual Firestore/Cloud Storage cost per active Free account (reads/writes/GB, not just guesswork) before fixing the attachment cap number in §3.1/§5 — the ~50–100-image figure is illustrative, not measured.
    • Add a photo/odometer attachment quota to Cloud Storage rules + client gate for Free accounts, and add image compression/downscaling on upload for both tiers.
    • Gate InferencePreference by entitlement once Stripe sync exists (§3.2) — CLOUD_ONLY should require Pro, and Free's PREFER_ON_DEVICE cloud-fallback path needs its own monthly quota with graceful degradation, not a silent uncapped cost today. This is arguably higher-priority than the storage cap, since it's a genuinely unbounded liability rather than a small predictable one.
    • Instrument what share of real devices actually run the on-device model vs. silently fall back to cloud under PREFER_ON_DEVICE — the whole §3 pricing argument assumes this is the common case, and that's currently an assumption, not a measured fact.
    • Build the formatted CPA-ready export (PDF with risk annotations) — currently only raw CSV exists.
    • Decide annual-discount mechanics in Stripe (this proposal assumes $99/yr on Pro, a ~17% discount vs. $9.99×12, matching Field Ledger's own annual-discount rate even though the base price is lower).
    • Update branding/landing_v0/site's pricing section and i18n.js (both en and zh — parity required) to drop the Portfolio card and the $19 price once this is decided.
